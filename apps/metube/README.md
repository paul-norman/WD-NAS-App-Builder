# MeTube App

[MeTube](https://github.com/alexta69/metube) downloads videos and audio from YouTube and [many other sites](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/supportedsites.md) *(with [yt-dlp](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp))*. This app adds the **Watch page** in front of it, made for watching something once or twice rather than building a library: everyone in the household has their own videos, a player, and a clean-up that deletes what's been watched. It runs natively on the NAS *(no Docker)*, with its own Python.

- **The Watch page** is at `https://videos.<domain>`, through the [Nginx app](../nginx/README.md) *(added automatically)* - by default only that way. Without the Nginx app *(or with that setting off, on the page)* it's also at `http://<nas-ip>:8086` on the local network, over plain HTTP. Each person logs in, pastes a link *(video, in MP4 with H.264 where there is one - what browsers, iPhones and TVs play - or audio only)*, sees its progress, and watches it there.
- **People are added on the management page** *(Apps → MeTube)*, each with a password *(given, or generated; shown once - only a PBKDF2 hash is kept)*. Each has their own folder in the download folder *(by default `Public/Watch Later/<name>`, with a thumbnail beside each video)*, and sees only their own videos and downloads. It's a household convenience, not privacy between its members: the NAS's admin can see every folder.
- **Playlists and channels**: a playlist's link adds its first 25 videos *(or 10, 50 or all of them - chosen beside the link)*, into a folder of its own named after it, numbered in its order (`<playlist>/001 - Title [Site id].mp4`). On the Watch page each playlist is one card *(its first video's thumbnail, how many videos, how many not watched)*; opening it shows only its videos, in its order *(with its own address, so the browser's Back goes back)*, with **Play all** *(from its first unwatched video, then on to the next as each ends)* and **Delete playlist** *(all but the kept videos)*; each video is still cleaned up on its own. **New playlist** makes one of a person's own: what they download while it's open goes into it *(a playlist's link too)*.
- **Renaming**: a video's title *(its playlist number and ID stay, its thumbnail is renamed with it, and it stays kept / watched)*, or a playlist's name. A media server sees a renamed video as a new one *(its own watched mark starts again)*. A video's link that happens to name a playlist (`watch?v=...&list=...`) adds just the video.
- **The player** plays at each person's own speed *(0.25× to 3×, in 0.25 steps; remembered for them, on any device)*.
- **SponsorBlock** *(each person's own settings, on the Watch page)*: the player skips the parts of YouTube videos that [SponsorBlock](https://sponsor.ajay.app/)'s viewers marked - sponsors, self-promotion and like / subscribe reminders by default, or intros, outros, previews, hooks, filler and non-music too - looked up each time a video plays *(by the first characters of a hash of its ID: SponsorBlock never learns which video it was)*. Downloads are named `Title [Site id]` for this *(the page shows only the title, with the site - YouTube, Vimeo and so on - as a small label; older `Title [id]` downloads count as YouTube)*. Videos YouTube refuses *(removed, private, not in this country, age-restricted)* say so in plain words under Downloading; **Clear failed** clears them all. Optionally, sponsors are cut out of new downloads instead *(MeTube's own option: for watching on a TV - permanent, cut at the nearest keyframe, and only what was marked by then)*; those videos aren't skipped in the player.
- **Clean-up**: a video is deleted 3 days after it was last watched, or 30 days after downloading if it was never watched *(the defaults: each person can change their own; 0 is never)*. **Keep** saves a video from it.
- **On a TV**: add the download folder to Plex, Jellyfin or Emby as a library - the whole folder *(one library, for a TV with a shared account)* or each person's folder as their own library *(private)*. Connected on the management page *(optional; its token or API key)*, the Watch page also counts what was watched there, by any of its accounts, for the clean-up. The app only reads from the media server: it never changes its libraries or users.
- **MeTube itself is never shown**: it listens on the NAS only *(`127.0.0.1:8085`)*, because its own page lists everyone's downloads.
- **yt-dlp is updated every night** from PyPI *(YouTube changes often, and older versions stop working; it can be turned off, or updated now, on the page)*: the new one is installed beside the packaged one *(`Nas_Prog/_metube/ytdlp`)*, checked, then used - the one in use stays if anything fails.
- It runs as its own user *(`metube`, ID 52, in the share group: the videos can be changed and deleted over SMB too)*. Its data is kept in `Nas_Prog/_metube`, across reinstalls and removal: its settings and users *(written by the page)*, MeTube's queue and history, each person's Watch state, the updated yt-dlp, its logs. The videos stay in the download folder.
- It's MeTube's source as released *(GitHub's archive of its tag, pinned by SHA256)* with its packages from its own lock *(`uv.lock`, each pinned by its checksums; Deno - the JavaScript runtime yt-dlp needs for YouTube - among them)*, its web interface built with Node.js, a portable Python 3.14 *(python-build-standalone)*, and its own small ffmpeg *(joining, remuxing, audio extraction, thumbnails - no network, no video encoders)*.
- For the Intel models only: Deno and curl-cffi have no 32-bit ARM builds. MeTube's Docker image also has bgutil-pot *(YouTube's "proof of origin" tokens)*: left out here, since its release needs a newer glibc than the NAS's - yt-dlp usually manages without it.

## Building the MeTube app

From the project root directory:

```bash
./build.sh metube
```

This first runs `static/metube` *(once per version)*. The WD bin files will be created in `packages/metube/`.

If you don't have docker installed, see the [guide](../../docker/README.md) in the `docker` directory. If you're on Windows 10 or 11, see the [Windows guide](../../docker/WINDOWS.md) for WSL / docker installation.
